# Motion: the words, then the freedom

Stages 2H, 3 and 3b. The planner reads this before writing the plan's action column, and every
builder brief carries the brief below as it is written, for the hook and for every card. Timing against the words
(in on the trigger word, out on the idea's last word) lives in `04c-timing.md`; this file is how
things move once they are on screen.

## Goal

Every visual performs its sentence. The viewer watches work being done on the real thing, moved
the way a senior motion designer would move it, and smooth enough that the eye never has to
search. From the first frame of the hook to the CTA, there is never a dead moment and never a
frantic one.

## Reasoning

The builder already knows motion design. Recipes (frame counts, a table of moves, one easing for
everything) make it copy and go stiff, and every video starts to look like the last one. What it
needs is the register, meaning the few exact words the craft uses, plus the bar and the freedom to
invent inside it. So this file names the principles and stops there. Numbers appear only where
the creator's pack states them as taste.

Retention holds on action. A card that shows a thing is read once and dropped. A card where
something is happening to that thing (a month sweeping past, a plan turning into a to-do list
that keeps growing, dead frames dropping out of a strip) is watched to its end, because the
viewer wants to see where it lands.

## The brief (given to every builder, as it is written)

> **Direct it like a film.** Before anything moves, imagine the story as something you could
> shoot. What would this sentence look like if it were real and you had a camera in the room?
> Build that world, fresh for each video.
>
> - **Make the abstract physical.** Every idea becomes an object with weight that someone could
>   hold, open, break or hand over. Prefer the object that performs the mechanism over a symbol
>   that stands for the word.
> - **Make numbers literal.** Show the scale itself, every one of them.
> - **Cause and effect in one shot.** The hook is the trailer: who, how much, what happens, and
>   to whom, in one continuous move.
> - **A cast, not slides.** One thing or character runs through the whole piece and changes with
>   the story.
> - **Light is emotion.** Colour temperature, glow and darkness tell the viewer how to feel before
>   the words do.
> - **The most real medium for each shot:** real footage and real marks, a redrawn interface at
>   its own geometry, or a world built in code with depth, light and material when the shot
>   stages a scene rather than proves a thing.
>
> Ask what nobody else would think of for this exact story, then make it inevitable.
>
> Then move it. Work like the senior motion designer on an Apple keynote film or a Stripe or Linear launch
> video: every move is motivated by the sentence, and the eye is led, never chased.
>
> - **Animate the verb.** Find the action in the sentence and perform it on the real thing. The
>   viewer watches work being done.
> - **Staging.** One hero per moment, a clear silhouette, a strong hierarchy. Everything else
>   supports it or gets out of the way.
> - **Timing and spacing.** Confident ease-out into rest and ease-in to leave, never linear, never
>   floaty. Landings are critically damped, with overshoot only where the material would give it.
> - **Weight.** Mass and inertia, anticipation before a big move, follow-through and overlapping
>   action after it, and secondary action on the parts.
> - **Arcs.** Anything thrown, swung or dealt travels on an arc.
> - **Depth.** Real z-space and perspective, parallax between layers, contact shadows that soften
>   with height, one consistent light.
> - **Motion blur** as from a 180° shutter, only while something is truly fast.
> - **Continuity.** Match cuts and morphs: the thing on screen becomes the next thing. The object
>   persists, and a change of state happens to what is already there.
> - **Rhythm.** Build, land, breathe. Offset and cascade siblings. Never everything at once,
>   never everything on one beat.
> - **Alive at rest.** While a finished state reads, it breathes: a slow push, a drift, a live
>   number.
> - **Material.** Real surfaces rendered with finish (paper, glass, metal, a real interface at its
>   own geometry), real content, square cards.
> - **Restraint.** Calm confidence over spectacle. Readable in one glance at phone width.
>
> These are the words, not the answer. Invent the move this sentence deserves, surprise us, and
> make it smooth.

## What good looks like

- With the sound off, the action column of the plan could be retold from the screen alone.
- Each card has one big move landed on its word, then a finished state that reads while it still
  breathes.
- Two neighbouring cards hand over by a move (one becomes the next, or it is carried off),
  never a hard cut, except into a bare run-up.
- No two cards in a video move the same way.

## Traps

- A pile of cards, a feed, a split-screen story, a notification scene, a UI narrated click by
  click: showing instead of doing.
- Coloured bars, grey rows, placeholder blocks, a symbol standing in for the word.
- Bounce and jitter as decoration; blur on a slow move; everything arriving on the same frame.
- A move so fast it cannot be followed, or a finished picture frozen with nothing alive in it.
- Copying a move from an earlier video because it worked there. Watch the references for the
  bar, then invent for this sentence.
