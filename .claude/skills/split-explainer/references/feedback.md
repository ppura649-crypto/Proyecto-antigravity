# What the creator said, and what it means

Every note below came from reviewing real exports of this format and of the explainer videos whose
visuals set its bar. Read it before planning and put the relevant notes into every builder brief, in
the creator's words, each turned into a rule. They are the standard, not suggestions.

## Layout and modes

- "way more cuts, between him full, then overlay full and overlay on top, like much more dynamic ...
  don't do it every 2-3 seconds, but make it depending on the context!! make it dynamic and nice."
  Change mode when the argument turns; most passages turn within a few seconds.
- "have him talking saying [the opening line] then an ultra well done motion of the avatar going down
  and then have the animations on top but super smoothly." The hook opens on the presenter, then he
  drops into the panel in one eased move and the visual arrives above him as he settles.
- "the transitions it's not smooth, when the bottom disappears ... for 2-3 frames the head comes back."
  Check every mode change frame by frame; no frame may flash the presenter back.
- "make sure when full there's nothing else around at any frame." A full visual owns the whole frame.
- "never half half for full visual." One ground for the whole 1080x1920; if it is dark, captions go white.
- "light green background for the avatar" (after trying his real room and a dark green). The presenter
  panel colour is a look choice; keep it clean behind the cut-out.

## The cut

- "the cuts are too hard, it's a bit weird, the words are cut." Never clip a word or its tail, in any
  language; keep natural breaths where a hard cut sounds abrupt.

## The visuals

- "they're not clear AT ALL ... much more improved, more creative and clear, more illustrative."
- "the graph are too complex, his audience is non technical." No axes, gridlines, log lines, HTTP text,
  JSON or dashboards for a general audience. One idea readable in one glance, sound off.
- "AI means like claude logo and chatgpt logo." Real companies and products appear as their real marks.
- "right now there's too many things on the screen ... we have 3 things ... it should be clear."
  One hero at a time; show only the two things the sentence names.
- "[this beat] should be much better ... make it super super creative." A turning point in the argument
  deserves the strongest picture of the passage.
- "they're animations we can barely see." Objects big (900 px or more wide), text big, centred.
- "not realistic, very weird, looks vibe coded" and "doesn't look realistic, not real interfaces" (redacted
  bars, line-art devices, tiny white cards). Real material: real marks, real copy, depth, light, finish.
- "we're reusing AGAIN [the same pair of marks]." Every idea gets a new main picture.
- "it's blurry, we see no animation, nothing moving." Blur is never the event; draw the mechanism moving.
- "slightly TOO energetic, it's hard to follow" and "the visuals are changing too quickly ... we don't even
  get to see the end of the animation." Constantly moving, but one move at a time, each finished and seen
  (hold about 0.7 s or more) before the next.
- "don't do rounded [cards]." Square card corners; real interface parts keep their own shape.
- "instead of bars colored ... make it more realistic." Every row of a drawn interface has real content.
- Loved: a month sweeping past in 3D, a scan lifting a photo into layers, a page folding into ads, a
  counter racing under a real mark, a real app window with real numbers, a 3D cutaway of a machine
  with live values, lockers filling, a pass turning gold. One physical action on real material, with
  weight, depth and light.

## The hook

- "the best first 3-4 seconds, like 5 different hooks but with insane motion like stop scrolling,
  highly contextualized ... don't copy it, but do something like that." See `hooks.md`.
- Hooks that lost: a literal UI story, a feed, a pile of cards, a notifications scene, a split-screen story,
  a generic effect that could open any video.

## Sound

- "add soft sfx, nice ones." Then: "i can't hear them?" Soft, but clearly audible. See `sound.md`.
