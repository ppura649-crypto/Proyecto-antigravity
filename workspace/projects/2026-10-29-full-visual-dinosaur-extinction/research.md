# Research: Dinosaur Extinction (checked 2026-10-29)

## Thesis
The Chicxulub asteroid explosion itself did not wipe out non-avian dinosaurs globally; a sequence of worst-case physical conditions (outer-solar-system carbonaceous composition, a steep ~60-degree impact angle, micrometer-sized silicate dust shutting down photosynthesis for nearly 2 years during a 15-year impact winter, and a Northern Hemisphere spring strike) collapsed the global food chain from the bottom up.

## Verified Primary Sources (checked 2026-10-29)

1. **Origin beyond Jupiter (Carbonaceous C-type asteroid, ~66 million years ago)**
   - **Study:** Mario Fischer-Gödde et al. (2024), *"Ruthenium isotopes show the Chicxulub impactor was a carbonaceous-type asteroid"*, *Science*, Vol. 385, Issue 6710, pp. 752–756.
   - **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk4868
   - **Verified facts:** Ruthenium (Ru) isotope signatures across Cretaceous-Paleogene (K-Pg) boundary clay samples match carbonaceous (C-type) asteroids that formed in the outer Solar System beyond the orbit of Jupiter, ruling out a comet origin.

2. **Impactor size (~12 km), Chicxulub crater (~180–200 km in Yucatán, Mexico), and worst-case ~60-degree impact angle**
   - **Study:** Gareth S. Collins et al. (2020), *"A steeply-inclined trajectory for the Chicxulub impact"*, *Nature Communications*, 11, 1480.
   - **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-15269-x
   - **Verified facts:** 3D numerical impact simulations matched to geophysical observations at Chicxulub (Yucatán coast, Mexico) show the ~12-km impactor struck from the northeast at a steeply inclined angle of 45°–60° (~60°) to the horizontal — a worst-case trajectory that maximized the volume of vaporized sulfur and pulverized target rock ejected into the upper atmosphere.

3. **Micrometer silicate dust (0.8–8.0 µm), 15-year atmospheric lifetime, up to 15 °C cooling, nearly 2-year photosynthesis shutdown, ~75% species extinction**
   - **Study:** Cem Berk Senel et al. (2023), *"Chicxulub impact winter sustained by fine silicate dust"*, *Nature Geoscience*, 16, 1033–1040.
   - **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-023-01290-4
   - **Verified facts:** Fine micrometer-sized silicate dust (~0.8–8.0 µm, finer than typical household smoke particles) measured from North Dakota K-Pg boundary sediments remained suspended in the atmosphere for up to 15 years, cooled global surface temperatures by up to 15 °C, and shut down global photosynthesis for nearly 2 years (~730 days), collapsing food chains and contributing to the extinction of ~75% of Earth's species.

4. **Season of impact: Boreal spring (Northern Hemisphere spring / Southern Hemisphere autumn)**
   - **Study:** Melanie A. D. During et al. (2022), *"The Mesozoic terminated in boreal spring"*, *Nature*, 603, 91–94.
   - **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04446-1
   - **Verified facts:** Fossilized paddlefish and sturgeons at the Tanis site in North Dakota — preserved with primary impact glass spherules trapped directly inside their gills — showed bone growth rings and carbon isotope records proving the impact occurred during Northern Hemisphere spring (boreal spring), catching northern ecosystems during active reproduction and growth while many southern-hemisphere and burrowing animals were entering autumn dormancy.
