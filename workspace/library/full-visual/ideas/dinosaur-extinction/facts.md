# Facts, dinosaur-extinction (checked 2026-10-29)

- **66 million years ago, ~12-kilometer asteroid, Yucatán coast of Mexico (~180–200 km Chicxulub crater), ~60-degree impact angle (45°–60° to horizontal), worst-case trajectory maximizing ejected rock and climate-altering sulfur gases:**
  Gareth S. Collins et al. (2020), "A steeply-inclined trajectory for the Chicxulub impact", *Nature Communications* 11, 1480. URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-15269-x
- **2024 ruthenium isotopes in K-Pg boundary clay proved carbonaceous (C-type) asteroid originating beyond Jupiter's orbit:**
  Mario Fischer-Gödde et al. (2024), "Ruthenium isotopes show the Chicxulub impactor was a carbonaceous-type asteroid", *Science* 385(6710), 752–756. URL: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk4868
- **2023 *Nature Geoscience* study: pulverized granite/silicate rock formed micrometer-sized silicate dust (0.8–8.0 µm), remained suspended in the atmosphere for up to 15 years, cooled global surface temperatures by up to 15 °C, shut down global photosynthesis for nearly 2 years (~730 days), collapsing food webs in the mass extinction of ~75% of all species:**
  Cem Berk Senel et al. (2023), "Chicxulub impact winter sustained by fine silicate dust", *Nature Geoscience* 16, 1033–1040. URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-023-01290-4
- **Fossil fish at Tanis, North Dakota with impact glass spherules trapped in their gills, bone growth rings and isotope records proved impact struck during Northern Hemisphere spring (boreal spring / austral autumn), catching northern ecosystems during breeding/rearing while southern/burrowing lineages hunkered down for autumn:**
  Melanie A. D. During et al. (2022), "The Mesozoic terminated in boreal spring", *Nature* 603, 91–94. URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04446-1
