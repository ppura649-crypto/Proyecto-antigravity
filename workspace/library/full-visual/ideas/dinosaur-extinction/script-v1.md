   - Fossil fish in North Dakota with impact glass in their gills (*Nature*, 2022) proved it struck in northern spring.

## 3. Script (200 words, ~58.8 s at 3.4 words/s)

The asteroid blast didn't kill the dinosaurs, 2 years without sunlight did, let me explain why.

Ok so 66 million years ago, a 12-kilometer asteroid slammed into Mexico's Yucatan coast. In 2024, ruthenium isotopes proved your killer rock came from beyond Jupiter.

Basically, 3D crater simulations showed it hit at a steep 60-degree angle. That was the deadliest angle you could pick, launching maximum sulfur and rock skyward.

Pulverized rock turned into microscopic silicate dust, finer than smoke in your kitchen. A 2023 Nature Geoscience study found that dust stayed trapped in the sky for 15 years. We're talking about a cloud that cooled Earth by up to 15 degrees Celsius.

Even if you survived the crash, photosynthesis across the planet shut down for nearly 2 years. That collapsed the food chain from the bottom up, wiping out 75% of all species.

Fossil fish in North Dakota with glass in their gills proved it hit in northern spring. Northern dinosaurs got caught outside in spring, while burrowing animals in southern autumn survived.

It wasn't one explosion, it was 730 days without the sun.

## 4. Facts used
- 66 million years ago, ~12 km asteroid, Yucatán coast, ~60° worst-case angle: Collins et al., 2020 (*Nature Communications*, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-15269-x)
- 2024 ruthenium isotopes, carbonaceous asteroid beyond Jupiter: Fischer-Gödde et al., 2024 (*Science*, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk4868)
- 2023 *Nature Geoscience*, microscopic silicate dust, 15 years suspended, up to 15 °C cooling, nearly 2 years (~730 days) photosynthesis shutdown, ~75% species extinction: Senel et al., 2023 (*Nature Geoscience*, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-023-01290-4)
- North Dakota (Tanis) fossil fish with impact glass spherules in gills, northern spring vs. southern autumn selective survival: During et al., 2022 (*Nature*, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04446-1)
