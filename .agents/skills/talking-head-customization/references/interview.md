# The preference conversation

The objective is a useful description of the creator, not a completed form. Reuse what they
already answered. Offer two or three concrete directions at a time; the examples below are
illustrations, not defaults to install. Ask follow-ups only when the answer affects a decision.

## Audience and purpose

Start with who is watching and what they should understand, feel or do. Ask which examples
the creator likes and **what** they like about them: readability, wit, calm pacing, detail,
trust, energy, a specific opening. A reference can teach one property without becoming a
full style-copy request.

Example: "I explain development tools to beginners. I want it clear and curious, with real
workflows, and I dislike shouting." This guides hooks and objects without specifying every
frame. If all they have is a color and a font, save those and leave the broader direction open.

## Hook

Ask what the first seconds should do, how assertive the writing may be, and which composition
they want: a short payoff sentence with normal captions, a spoken-word stack, a picture with
captions, or their own combination. Ask whether pictures should wait until the title leaves.
Keep copy choice separate from visual layout and from the recorded spoken opening.

Explain: the purpose is to give viewers a reason to stay and make the promise readable quickly.
That purpose does not require everyone to use the original creator's stack or an early object.

Illustrative copy for a video that demonstrates one command sorting files:
"One command sorts this folder" promises a result. "File organization" only names the topic.
Do not invent the command, number or outcome. Ask about claims, humor, case, title size and
duration as preferences where they matter, then verify fit on actual footage.

## Body captions and typography

Ask separately about word-by-word subtitles versus short phrases, case, punctuation,
emphasis frequency, normal versus hero words, font family and weight, shadow or backing, and
any access/readability needs. Explain that phrases favor reading a thought together, while
word-by-word captions follow delivery more closely. Neither choice permits missing words.

Ask whether the opening should use the same treatment. A choice for big words in the hook
does not automatically apply heroes throughout the body. Clarify whether a font change covers
all authored text, only captions, or captions plus designed-card labels. Preserve typography
inside recognizable source artifacts unless the creator explicitly asks otherwise.

Placement is a preference within space measured from this person's footage. Do not ask them
to choose a remembered baseline percentage or a size detached from the output dimensions.

## Colors, with an explicit relationship

Ask for base caption text, important-word color, title color if different, designed-card
background, card text and card accent. Accept names, hex values or a visual reference; propose
exact values for ambiguous names and show them in the read-back before treating them as settled.

Ask whether captions and cards share one accent or use distinct accents. Example: white words
`#FFFFFF`, blue emphasis `#3B82F6`, navy plates `#0B1220`, white card labels, and the same blue
for authored chart highlights. The payoff is a coherent reading hierarchy; blue is not the goal.

Explain that color must remain legible against their footage. A pale-room user may want dark
ink or a light/dark backing. A contrast fix should preserve the chosen identity and touch only
the affected object. Real logos, app surfaces and source images do not get recolored by this.

## Cards

Ask about surfaces: dark, paper, glass, or frameless; corners, borders, shadows and density.
White cards do not mean every idea should be a white text box: the surface dresses a picture,
it never chooses it.

What the picture is (a real page, a workflow replica, a drawn mechanism, a chart) is not asked
here. The Talking Head skill decides that from the sentence, the same way for every creator. If
they raise it, say so, and record only what concerns the surface.

## Cut pacing

Ask about hard, tight delivery versus retaining more breath or conversational air.
Keep complete words and natural voice intact. Do not turn an example timing into a universal
cut point or alter playback speed because the creator asked for energy.

How a picture moves and how much of the video pictures cover are not asked here: they follow
the words, by the Talking Head skill's own patterns.

## Sound

Distinguish the recorded voice from added SFX and background music. "No sounds" may mean no
added effects, not a silent talking head. Ask about sparse effects, their palette and loudness,
music if wanted, and voice cleanup. Defaults should reuse an approved choice on later videos.

Explain that sound should mark meaning and leave speech intelligible. If new SFX are wanted,
let the creator hear a small palette before treating it as their permanent preference. Never
inherit another creator's palette or imply an unheard file was approved.

## CTA and post copy

Ask the desired ending and what action it supports: save, follow, a comment keyword, or none.
Keep it tied to the creator's actual offer. Ask separately about written post-caption voice,
length, language, links and hashtags if post copy is part of what they want saved.

How the work is reviewed is not asked here: the Talking Head skill decides where it stops. A
saved preference never authorizes publishing or sending anything.

## One read-back and later learning

Show: "Your goal is ... The opening will ... Subtitles will ... Designed cards can ... Real
interfaces keep ... Added sound will ... I will ask you again only for ..."
Give one opening example based on a real topic, or label it hypothetical.
Ask whether to save it as their default. Allow corrections before marking setup complete.

Each choice needs a short rationale that another editor can apply. If the creator gave only
the choice, write "editorial interpretation" beside the proposed reason. Do not claim the
creator said it. Unresolved taste can remain open without blocking settled colors and fonts.
